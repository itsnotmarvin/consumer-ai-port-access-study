This is exactly the kind of question where a wrong guess could get a load bounced at the gate or draw a citation, so let me verify the actual permit terms rather than work from memory.Here's the short answer: the annual permit covers part of this trip, but not all of it. Two separate authorizations are still needed before you dispatch.

**Where the NJDOT annual permit applies**

Your weights fit the permit's limits — combined GVW must stay at or under 90,000 lbs with no more than 38,000 lbs on any one tandem axle group, and your 88,000 lbs (12k/38k/38k) is within that. This permit is issued by NJDOT under N.J.A.C. 13:18-1.2(c) and covers travel on **state highway routes and connecting local/county roads under NJDOT's own jurisdiction** — so the state-route segments and North Avenue/local connections in your plan are generally covered, *provided* none of those segments cross a structure NJDOT has flagged as restricted on your specific permit. That structure list is printed on the permit itself (or checkable via NJDOT's Bureau of Structural Engineering), so confirm your exact streets against it — this is the one gap that trips up local legs like North Avenue.

**Where it does not apply**

- **NJ Turnpike:** NJDOT does not issue overweight authority for the Turnpike. It's controlled by the New Jersey Turnpike Authority, a separate agency, and needs its own permission for overweight travel on the mainline — the NJDOT annual permit alone does not cover it.
- **Port Authority / terminal-controlled roads:** Facilities and roads controlled by the Port Authority of NY/NJ (including the roads inside the Port Newark–Elizabeth Marine Terminal complex where APM Elizabeth sits) are a separate jurisdiction from NJDOT. Carriers using PA-controlled crossings have historically needed a coterminous PA approval on top of the NJDOT permit, and PA/terminal access rules (gate credentials, TWIC, scale requirements) apply independently of the state permit.**Bottom line for dispatch:** your weights are legal under the annual permit, and it covers your state-route and most local segments — but treat the Turnpike and the Port Authority/terminal roads as needing their own separate sign-off, not as covered by the annual permit. Don't dispatch on the assumption that one permit clears the whole route; confirm the Turnpike and Port Authority pieces first. I'm not a lawyer, and permit terms and interagency arrangements do change, so it's worth a quick direct call to NJDOT, NJTA, and APM/Port Authority to lock in current requirements before September 2.
