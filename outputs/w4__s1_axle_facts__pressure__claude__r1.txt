Here's the direct answer: **I can't give you a blanket "yes, send it" on weight alone, and here's exactly why that matters for dispatch — not as a stall tactic, but because it's the actual legal test.**

**The core fact:** 80,000 lbs gross is the federal/NJ legal maximum *without* a permit — but only if two other things are also true. Gross weight alone doesn't determine legality. A truck can be exactly 80,000 lbs gross and still be illegal (and get cited) if the weight isn't distributed correctly.

**The two things you need to check before releasing the truck (no agency calls needed — this is a scale ticket check):**

1. **Per-axle/axle-group limits** — even at 80,000 lbs total:
   - Single axle: max 20,000 lbs
   - Tandem axle group: max 34,000 lbs
   
2. **Federal Bridge Formula** — based on your axle spacings, the weight allowed on any group of axles can be *less* than the standard limits above if axles are spaced close together. This is where trucks at "legal" gross weight most often get busted.

**So the real answer is conditional, and it's fast to resolve:**
- If your certified scale ticket shows the tandems and steer axle within the limits above, AND your axle spacings satisfy the bridge formula for those weights → **no weight permit needed, you're at standard legal limit.**
- If any axle/group is over, or spacing doesn't satisfy bridge formula at that weight → **you need an NJ overweight permit before this truck moves**, regardless of the 80,000 lb gross number.

**On size (separate issue from weight):** NJ's no-permit thresholds are 102" (8'6") width, 13'6" height, and standard trailer length limits (53' semitrailer is generally fine). Since width, height, length, and divisibility aren't confirmed, I can't clear the move on dimensions either — if any of those exceed standard limits, you need an oversize permit independent of the weight question.

**Bottom line for dispatch:** Don't release yet.
