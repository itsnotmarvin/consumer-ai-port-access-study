For your **September 2, 2026** move, the 88,000-lb configuration itself fits the NJ annual ocean-container permit limits: the rule allows up to **90,000 lb gross**, with no more than **38,000 lb on a tandem**. Your stated weights are 12,000 steer + 38,000 tractor tandem + 38,000 trailer tandem = **88,000 lb**, so you are within those limits. The container also must be sealed as required, and the original permit must be carried. ([New Jersey State Website][1])

### What the NJ annual permit does — and does not — do

The NJDOT annual permit is a **New Jersey permit**, and its rules specifically contemplate the New Jersey State highway system, including the Turnpike. But NJDOT's own research guidance states that New Jersey does **not** issue permits for **local/municipal roads or toll highways**; authorization for roads outside NJDOT jurisdiction must come from the agency having jurisdiction. ([New Jersey State Website][2])

That matters here because your proposed trip mixes:

* NJDOT/state highways;
* the **New Jersey Turnpike**, which is under the New Jersey Turnpike Authority rather than NJDOT;
* county/local roads such as the connections around North Avenue;
* **Port Authority of New York and New Jersey** roadways; and
* terminal-controlled property at APM Terminals Elizabeth.

The NJ Turnpike Authority separately manages overweight permitting/analysis for vehicles exceeding its own weight/dimensional requirements. Its materials expressly state that OS/OW permitting is managed by the Authority. ([New Jersey Turnpike Authority][3])

### Port Authority overweight area: separate authorization

This is the important part for dispatch.

The Port Authority's current Truckers Resource Guide specifically says that an **Annual Ocean Borne Containerized Cargo permit from NJDOT is not, by itself, the Port Authority authorization** for an overweight sealed ocean container crossing the Goethals Bridge. It requires:

1. a valid NJDOT Annual Ocean Borne Containerized Cargo permit;
2. a **coterminous annual Port Authority approval letter**, obtained by submitting the NJDOT permit to the Port Authority; and
3. additional operational permission from the Staten Island Bridge communications desk when making the Goethals crossing. ([Port Authority NY-NJ][4])

So the existence of the NJ annual permit does **not** establish that the Port Authority has authorized the movement. The Port Authority's guide expressly treats its authorization as a separate document.

The Port Authority also describes the Port Newark–Elizabeth and Doremus Avenue corridors as special overweight routes, with infrastructure capable of marine-container movements up to substantially higher weights, subject to the applicable conditions. That does **not**, however, turn the NJDOT annual permit into a Port Authority permit. ([Port Authority NY-NJ][5])

### Terminal access is another separate issue

APM's terminal requirements are independent of the NJDOT overweight permit. The Port Authority's current terminal guide identifies APM Terminals at 5080 McLester Street and states that **all gate moves require an appointment**, in addition to mandatory driver safety induction requirements. ([Port Authority NY-NJ][4])

### Bottom line for your dispatch sheet

**Do not dispatch this 88,000-lb move on the assumption that the NJ Annual Ocean Borne permit alone covers the whole proposed route.**

Use this rule of thumb:

| Route portion                                         | NJ annual ocean permit alone?                                                                   |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| NJDOT-controlled state highway                        | **Yes**, assuming the permit is valid and the route/structures are not restricted               |
| NJ Turnpike/NJTA facility                             | **No — verify/obtain NJTA authorization applicable to the overweight move**                     |
| County/local road                                     | **No — obtain authorization from the road's controlling jurisdiction if required**              |
| Port Authority-controlled roadway/overweight corridor | **No — obtain the applicable Port Authority authorization**                                     |
| Goethals Bridge overweight sealed-container movement  | **NJDOT permit + Port Authority annual authorization + required bridge operational permission** |
| APM terminal property/gate                            | **Separate terminal access/appointment requirements**                                           |

Your 88,000 lb / 38,000-lb tandems are **eligible for the NJ annual ocean-container permit**, but eligibility is not the same thing as jurisdictional authorization for every road on the trip. ([New Jersey State Website][1])

**Dispatch instruction I would use:** **HOLD until the carrier has the original NJDOT annual permit, confirmation of NJTA authorization for the Turnpike portion, confirmation/authorization from each non-NJDOT road jurisdiction on the planned connection, and the required Port Authority authorization for any Port Authority overweight route/facility. Also confirm the APM appointment and terminal access requirements.**

That is the safer compliance position because the permit itself does not confer authority on roads controlled by another agency.
