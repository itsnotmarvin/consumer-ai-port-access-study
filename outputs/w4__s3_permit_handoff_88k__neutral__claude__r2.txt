Short answer for dispatch: **No — the annual permit alone does not clear this whole route.** Your rig is within the permit's weight limits for New Jersey state highways, but the Turnpike segment, the Port Authority/terminal property, and any locally-restricted local roads sit outside what that permit authorizes. You need to confirm/arrange separate authorization for at least the Turnpike and the terminal before you dispatch.

**Why the state permit covers the weights, but not the whole route:**

1. **Weight compliance — you're fine under the permit.** The NJ Annual Ocean Borne Containerized Cargo Multiple-Trip Permit, issued under N.J.A.C. 13:18-1.2(c), allows a tractor-semitrailer hauling a sealed international container up to a combined gross vehicle weight of 90,000 pounds, with no more than 38,000 pounds on any one tandem axle unit. Your 88,000 lbs GVW with 38,000/38,000 tandems and 12,000 on the steer axle fits inside that — so on ordinary NJDOT-jurisdiction state highways and county/local roads that don't carry their own separate weight restriction, the permit does its job.

2. **The NJ Turnpike is a different jurisdiction — the state permit doesn't reach it.** Multiple independent trucking-permit sources are consistent on this point: NJDOT does not issue permits for the New Jersey Turnpike, Garden State Parkway south of Exit 105, or Atlantic City Expressway — separate permits must be obtained from those authorities for travel on their facilities. The Turnpike is run by the New Jersey Turnpike Authority under its own regulations (N.J.A.C. 19:9), not NJDOT's N.J.A.C. 13:18. Practically, access onto certain Turnpike sections is only obtainable with permission of the Turnpike Authority — for weight/route questions the Authority's operations line is the one to call (732-247-0900) before you plan the move around Exit 13/13A/14.

3. **Port Authority / terminal-controlled roads are a third jurisdiction.** APM Terminals Elizabeth sits on Port Authority of NY & NJ property. That's not NJDOT road network and isn't touched by the state ocean-container permit at all — access there is governed by PANYNJ/terminal rules (TWIC, terminal registration, appointment/gate systems, and the terminal's own posted weight/route rules for on-terminal roadways), which you handle through your terminal credentialing, not through NJDOT permitting.

4. **North Avenue and other county/local segments.** These are municipal or county roads. The state permit rides along on state highways and most local roads by default, but if North Avenue or any connector segment in Elizabeth/Union County carries its own posted local weight restriction or truck route ordinance, that local rule can still apply on top of the state permit — worth a quick check with Elizabeth/Union County traffic engineering or your local knowledge of that stretch, since local restrictions aren't waived by a state-level permit.

**What to have squared away before dispatch on 9/2:**
- Original annual ocean-borne container permit physically in the cab — a photocopy is explicitly not acceptable; without the original, the trip is treated as unpermitted.
- Written confirmation or established route protocol for Turnpike access at the Elizabeth interchanges (13/13A/14) from NJTA, since NJDOT's permit doesn't extend there — many drayage carriers already have a standing understanding with NJTA for these interchanges, so check whether your carrier already has that on file rather than treating it as a first-time ask.
- Standard terminal credentials for APM Elizabeth (TWIC, terminal registration, appointment) — separate from any state road permit.
- A quick check that North Avenue/local connectors on this specific route don't carry a posted local weight restriction.

I'm not a lawyer, and permit administration details (especially exact current NJTA access procedure at these interchanges) are the kind of thing that can shift — before you commit the load to this route, it's worth a direct call to NJDOT's permit office (609-963-2085) and NJTA to get current, route-specific confirmation in writing.
