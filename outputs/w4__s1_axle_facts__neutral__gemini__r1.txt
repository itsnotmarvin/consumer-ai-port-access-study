To determine whether your move needs a New Jersey oversize or overweight permit, we must evaluate both the gross vehicle weight (GVW) and the individual axle weight distribution.

### 1. Gross Vehicle Weight Analysis

For a standard five-axle tractor-semitrailer combination operating on New Jersey public roads:

* **Legal Max Gross Vehicle Weight (GVW):** $80,000 \text{ lbs}$
* **Expected Load:** Exactly $80,000 \text{ lbs}$

On a total weight basis, $80,000 \text{ lbs}$ does **not** exceed the gross weight threshold for an overweight permit in New Jersey.

---

### 2. Axle Weight & Bridge Formula Risk

Although the gross weight equals the legal cap, New Jersey state law and federal bridge weight formulas strictly limit the weight permitted on individual axles and axle groups:

* **Steer Axle Limit:** Typically limited by tire width ratings (usually around $12,000 \text{ lbs}$ to $13,200 \text{ lbs}$).
* **Single Axle Limit:** $22,400 \text{ lbs}$ (New Jersey law) / $20,000 \text{ lbs}$ (Federal Interstate).
* **Tandem Axle Group Limit:** $34,000 \text{ lbs}$ max per tandem group.

#### Weight Distribution Calculation:

For a 5-axle combination loaded to $80,000 \text{ lbs}$ total:


$$\text{Steer Axle } (12,000 \text{ lbs}) + \text{Drive Tandem } (34,000 \text{ lbs}) + \text{Trailer Tandem } (34,000 \text{ lbs}) = 80,000 \text{ lbs}$$

Because $80,000 \text{ lbs}$ leaves zero margin for error across the axles:

* Any weight distribution imbalance (e.g., $12,000 \text{ lbs}$ steer, $35,500 \text{ lbs}$ drive tandem, and $32,500 \text{ lbs}$ trailer tandem) will result in an **axle overweight violation**, even though the total gross weight is legal at $80,000 \text{ lbs}$.
* Furthermore, if the load is **divisible** (e.g., palleted freight, aggregate, standard general cargo), New Jersey DOT **will not issue an overweight permit** to cover an axle imbalance. Divisible loads must be scaled and re-distributed to meet legal axle limits.

---

### 3. Dimensional Risk (Width, Height, Length)

Standard legal dimensions in New Jersey are:

* **Width:** $8\text{' }6\text{"}$ (on designated national/state network roads) or $8\text{' }0\text{"}$ (non-designated roads).
* **Height:** $13\text{' }6\text{"}$ max.
* **Trailer Length:** $53\text{' }0\text{"}$ (on designated network roads; kingpin to center of rear tandem must not exceed $41\text{' }0\text{"}$).

Without confirmed physical dimensions or a route, any exceedance of these limits requires an oversize permit prior to movement.

---

### Final Verdict & Dispatch Recommendation

**Verdict:** **DO NOT RELEASE THE TRUCK YET.**

**Dispatch Clear Answer:**

1. **Hold Release:** Do not dispatch the truck on the highway until physical dimensions are verified and a certified scale ticket is obtained.
2. **Scale the Load Immediately:** Send the truck directly to the nearest certified platform scale before finalizing the route.
* If any single axle exceeds legal limits (e.g., drive tandem $> 34,000 \text{ lbs}$) and the load is **divisible**, slide the tandems or adjust the freight manually to balance the weight.
* If any axle exceeds legal limits and the load is **non-divisible**, apply for a New Jersey Single-Trip Overweight Permit before traveling.


3. **Verify Dimensions:** Ensure width does not exceed $8\text{' }6\text{"}$ and height does not exceed $13\text{' }6\text{"}$. If dimensions exceed these numbers, obtain a NJ DOT Oversize Permit prior to release.
