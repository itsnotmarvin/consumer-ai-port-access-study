**Dispatch answer: Do not release the truck yet.**

An exactly **80,000-lb gross** five-axle tractor-semitrailer is **not automatically overweight in New Jersey**, but 80,000 lb is the **statutory maximum gross weight**, not a blanket authorization to disregard axle-group or dimensional limits. New Jersey's rules also require the applicable axle/group limits to be satisfied. ([FHWA Operations][1])

Because you **do not yet know the individual axle/group weights, axle spacings, dimensions, load divisibility, permit status, or route**, there is presently not enough information to establish that this particular move is legal without a permit.

### What dispatch should require before release

1. **Certified scale weights** showing the steer axle, drive tandem, and trailer tandem (or whatever axle configuration applies).
2. **Axle spacings**, particularly the relevant distance between the first and last axles of the applicable group.
3. **Loaded dimensions** — width, height, and overall length.
4. **Load classification** — confirm whether the cargo is divisible or qualifies as a non-divisible load.
5. **Final route**, because Interstate and other New Jersey highways can have different applicable axle/group calculations and posted restrictions.
6. **Permit/registration documentation**, if any permit is required.

New Jersey's statutory framework sets, among other limits, **80,000 lb maximum gross weight**, **34,000 lb for a qualifying tandem axle**, and **22,400 lb for a qualifying single axle**; Interstate routes also use the federal bridge-formula-style axle-group table. Thus, knowing only that the total is 80,000 lb does **not** prove that the combination is legal. ([New Jersey Traffic Ticket Attorney][2])

There is also a New Jersey **5% enforcement tolerance** for certain weight violations, but it **does not increase the 80,000-lb maximum**; the statute expressly conditions the tolerance on the gross weight not exceeding 80,000 lb. I would **not use that tolerance as a basis for dispatching an unverified load**. ([New Jersey Legislature][3])

For size, New Jersey treats vehicles exceeding the statutory size/weight limits as OS/OW movements requiring the appropriate permit. NJDOT currently administers the permitting process through its SUPERLOAD system. ([New Jersey State Website][4])

**Important:** A standard commercial tractor-semitrailer carrying a **divisible commercial-move load** cannot simply obtain an ordinary overweight permit to make an otherwise overweight divisible load legal. New Jersey's OS/OW permitting framework is generally directed to qualifying **non-divisible** loads. ([GotPermits][5])
