**Dispatch answer: Do not release the truck yet.**

An exactly **80,000-lb gross** five-axle tractor-semitrailer is **not automatically overweight in New Jersey**, but 80,000 lb is only the maximum legal gross weight under the applicable axle/spacing and route rules. NJ’s rules determine allowable GVW using axle count and the **center-to-center spacing of the outer axles**; NJDOT guidance lists a five-axle combination at **80,000 lb when the applicable outer-axle spacing is 53 ft**, subject to the other axle limitations. ([New Jersey Department of Transportation][1])

More importantly, you currently lack information that is necessary to make the legal determination:

* **Axle weights / axle-group weights** — required to establish compliance with the individual axle limits.
* **Outer-axle spacing** — relevant to the allowable gross weight.
* **Width, height and length** — needed to determine whether the vehicle/load is dimensionally legal.
* **Load divisibility** — important because NJ's OS/OW permitting framework does not generally authorize overweight divisible loads. ([New Jersey State Website][2])
* **Final route** — important because permit applications are evaluated against the proposed route, and NJDOT can reject a configuration that overstresses structures on that route. ([New Jersey State Website][3])
* **Permit status** — if any applicable size or weight limit is exceeded, the required NJDOT permit must be obtained before travel. NJDOT is the agency issuing NJ oversize/overweight permits through its permitting system. ([New Jersey State Website][4])

### The key point for dispatch

**80,000 lb gross by itself does not establish that the truck is legal.** A five-axle combination can potentially be legal at 80,000 lb, but you cannot determine that from gross weight alone. If the axle configuration, spacing, or axle loading makes the combination exceed a statutory limit, it is overweight even though the scale's gross reading is exactly 80,000 lb. NJ State Police actively enforce the state's size-and-weight laws at fixed scales and with roving weight teams. ([New Jersey State Website][5])

Likewise, if the loaded dimensions exceed NJ's legal dimensions, an oversize issue exists independently of the 80,000-lb gross weight.

**Therefore, with the facts you have now, I would mark the dispatch status: `HOLD — DO NOT RELEASE`.**

Before releasing it for September 2, obtain at minimum:

1. Certified/verified **axle and axle-group weights**.
2. **Outer-axle spacing** and the complete axle configuration.
3. Loaded **width, height and overall length**.
4. Confirmation whether the cargo is **divisible or non-divisible**.
5. The **actual route**, including applicable NJ highways/roads.
6. Verification of whether an NJDOT **OS/OW permit is required and, if required, that it has been issued for that exact configuration/route/date**.

If those checks establish that the combination is within all applicable legal size, gross-weight, axle-weight and route restrictions, **no OS/OW permit is needed merely because the truck weighs exactly 80,000 lb**. If any applicable limit is exceeded, **do not release it until the appropriate NJDOT authorization is in hand**. ([New Jersey State Website][4])
