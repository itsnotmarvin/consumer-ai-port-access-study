**Clear dispatch answer: No.** The New Jersey Annual Ocean Borne Containerized Cargo Multiple-Trip Permit does **not** by itself authorize the entire trip, and you should **not dispatch on the assumption that it covers Port Authority-controlled roads/bridges or toll facilities without their separate approval.**

For your stated load, the NJ annual permit is otherwise within its basic weight envelope:

* Gross: **88,000 lb ≤ 90,000 lb**
* Tractor tandem: **38,000 lb ≤ 38,000 lb**
* Trailer tandem: **38,000 lb ≤ 38,000 lb**
* Sealed international ocean container: qualifying type
* Legal dimensions: consistent with the annual ocean-container permit's dimensional limitation. ([New Jersey State Website][1])

But there is an important limitation that answers your question directly: **N.J.A.C. 13:18-1.9 expressly says an overweight permit is not valid on a toll road without the express approval of the applicable tolling authority.** It specifically names the New Jersey Turnpike Authority for the New Jersey Turnpike and Garden State Parkway. ([New Jersey State Website][1])

### What that means for your September 2 dispatch

| Part of trip                                                  | NJ annual ocean-container permit alone?                                                      |
| ------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| NJ state/local roads where the permit is otherwise applicable | **Generally yes**, subject to routing/structural restrictions                                |
| **New Jersey Turnpike**                                       | **No — express NJ Turnpike Authority approval required**                                     |
| County/local connecting roads                                 | **Not automatically overridden**; applicable local routing/weight restrictions still control |
| Port Authority-controlled facility/bridge                     | **No — separate Port Authority requirements can apply**                                      |
| APM/Port Authority marine-terminal access                     | **No — terminal/Port Authority access requirements remain applicable**                       |

NJ's routing rules themselves distinguish the National Network, state/access-network roads and local roads; reaching a terminal can provide access to otherwise restricted roads in appropriate circumstances, but that does **not** turn a state overweight permit into a blanket authorization from every road owner. ([New Jersey State Website][2])

### Particularly important: Port Authority

The Port Authority's own trucker guidance is unusually explicit about overweight sealed ocean containers. For the **Goethals Bridge**, it says the process is:

1. Have the valid NJ Annual Ocean Borne Containerized Cargo permit.
2. **Obtain a separate, coterminous annual Port Authority authorization letter** by submitting the NJ permit to the Port Authority.
3. For the actual crossing, obtain the required crossing/time permission from the bridge communications desk. 

So the Port Authority treats the NJ permit and the Port Authority authorization as **two separate things**, not as one permit that automatically confers the other's authority.

The Port Authority's marine-terminal tariff also separately regulates overweight/overdimensional movement on marine-terminal highways. It allows containerized shipments up to **128,000 lb GVW**, subject to manufacturer safe-working-load limits, but that is a **Port Authority facility rule**, not a statement that an NJDOT permit grants access. 

### One important distinction

Your **88,000-lb** combination is legal under the NJ annual ocean-container permit's 90,000-lb ceiling, but that doesn't make it "legal everywhere." The annual permit itself says travel cannot use structures that NJDOT identifies as restricted for these ocean-container combinations; if such a route cannot be avoided, a **single-trip permit** is required. ([New Jersey State Website][1])

**Bottom line for dispatch:**

> **Do not dispatch this 88,000-lb load on the proposed route based solely on the NJ Annual Ocean Borne Containerized Cargo Multiple-Trip Permit. Before dispatch, confirm/obtain the NJ Turnpike Authority's express approval for the Turnpike portion and any required separate Port Authority authorization for the Port Authority-controlled portion, including the applicable bridge/terminal requirements. Also verify that the exact county/local route and the structures on it are authorized for the combination.**

The Port Authority's current trucker guide specifically says its overweight authorization process requires the NJ annual permit **plus** the Port Authority authorization; its guide was revised August 2025, so I would use that rather than assuming the NJ permit has superseded it.
