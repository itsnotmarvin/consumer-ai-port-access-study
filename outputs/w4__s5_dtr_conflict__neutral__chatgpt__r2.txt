**Dispatch answer: reject it unless the truck is already validly registered in the DTR and the DTR shows it as compliant for that VIN/vehicle.**

For **September 2, 2026**, a 2010 diesel is **not eligible for a new DTR registration**. The current Port Authority tariff says that, since July 2, 2023, only trucks with engines meeting or exceeding the **2014 heavy-duty diesel EPA standard** may complete DTR registration. ([Port Authority NY-NJ][1])

However, that does **not** mean every existing 2010 diesel was automatically barred on July 2, 2023. The Port Authority describes the 2014 rule as preventing the *universe of older trucks from expanding*; its 2023 annual report expressly says only trucks meeting the 2014 standard could register **as a new user**. ([New York-New Jersey Shipping Association][2])

The critical issue for your truck is therefore **its existing DTR status**, not merely its 2010 engine year.

### What this means for your September 2 move

* **If the VIN is currently in the DTR and the terminal can verify it as compliant:** a 2010 diesel can continue to be used under the existing registration/grandfathered status. The Port Authority's current rules focus terminal access on whether the truck can be verified as compliant through its DTR record. ([Port Authority NY-NJ][1])
* **If the truck is not currently verifiable as compliant in the DTR:** **do not dispatch it.** Subrule 34-1140 expressly prohibits terminal access for a truck that cannot be verified as compliant by its DTR records. ([Port Authority NY-NJ][1])
* **If the previous owner's DTR registration/RFID is still associated with the truck but the ownership information was never properly amended:** I would **not assume that is sufficient**. Subrule 34-1100 requires changes to registered-vehicle information to be amended through PortTruckPass within 10 calendar days, with supporting documentation potentially required. ([Port Authority Builds][3])
* Your **80,000-lb five-axle configuration and nonhazardous cargo don't create an exception** to the DTR requirement. The Port's definition covers the applicable Class 8 drayage operation, and the DTR contains the vehicle/VIN/engine/owner information used for verification. ([Port Authority NY-NJ][1])

**One important wrinkle:** the current tariff text of §34-1140 contains a clause saying a truck that is “not an Alternative Drayage Truck” cannot enter. Taken literally, that would exclude ordinary compliant diesel trucks too, which conflicts with the Port Authority's own 2014-diesel registration rule and its description of the 2023 program. The Port Authority's own truck-replacement material and DOE's current summary both describe the operative requirement as **2014-or-newer diesel**, with alternative-fuel trucks exempt. ([Alternative Fuels Data Center][4]) I therefore would **not interpret that clause as a blanket diesel ban**.

### Bottom line for dispatch

**Do not send this 2010 tractor to APM Elizabeth based merely on the fact that the previous owner may have had it registered.**

For the September 2, 2026 move:

> **2010 diesel + current DTR record showing the VIN/vehicle as compliant = dispatchable.**
> **2010 diesel + DTR status unknown, stale, ownership/RFID mismatch, or not verifiable = reject/hold the truck until PortTruckPass confirms the registration.**

The safest operational instruction is therefore **“DTR verification required before dispatch.”** A 2010 engine cannot be newly registered today, so if the old registration was lost or cannot be transferred/amended into a verifiable compliant DTR record, this tractor should not be used for the APM move.
